Inside Nursing Home Fire Safety — Finding Patterns in Citations

When families place loved ones in nursing homes, fire readiness is a core expectation. This analysis explores how fire-safety deficiencies appear across facilities and how predictive modeling can support earlier intervention.

View Full Notebook CMS Data Source

Questions of Interest

  1. Which states see the highest volumes of fire safety citations?
  2. What are the most common safety issues flagged during inspections?
  3. Can we predict whether a new violation may escalate into high severity?

Key Findings

State-by-State Volumes

California (CA) and Ohio (OH) show the highest absolute citation counts, reflecting major facility footprints and active inspection activity.

Most Frequent Deficiencies

Smoke deficiencies dominate the dataset (>78,000 cases), followed by gas, vacuum, and electrical system issues.

Severity Distribution

Most citations are moderate (Grades D, E, F). High-severity grades (G–L) are rare, representing less than 0.1% of records.

D-F: Common G-L: <0.1%

Model Performance & Caveat

A Random Forest classifier was trained to flag potential high-severity outcomes from state, deficiency category, inspection cycle count, and time-to-fix features.

Overall Accuracy

97.9%

High global classification rate across all classes.

Recall / Sensitivity
(High-Severity Class)

45%

Share of true high-severity cases captured.

Important: The dataset is highly imbalanced, so accuracy alone is not sufficient to evaluate model usefulness. Recall/sensitivity for rare high-severity cases is critical for operational decision-making.

Hypothetical Scenario

For a second-cycle inspection in California with a smoke deficiency and a projected 20-day resolution window, the model estimates:

  • 84.78% probability of Low-to-Moderate Severity
  • 15.22% probability of High Severity

This risk signal helps prioritize follow-up actions before outcomes worsen.

Takeaway

Data-informed oversight can shift safety management from reactive enforcement to proactive prevention. Combining frequent deficiency patterns with predictive risk scoring supports earlier intervention where residents may face elevated harm.

CRISP-DM Summary

  1. Business/Data Understanding: Identify major risk drivers in nursing home fire safety citations.
  2. Exploratory Data Analysis: Compare citation volume by state and category; smoke deficiencies emerged as most common.
  3. Modeling: Train a Random Forest classifier with class balancing to address severe class imbalance.
  4. Evaluation: Achieve 97.9% overall accuracy and 45% recall/sensitivity for rare high-severity violations.
