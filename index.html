Inside Nursing Home Fire Safety — Finding Patterns in Citations

This post summarizes notebook results from the CMS nursing-home fire-safety citations dataset. The analysis reviews where citations cluster, what deficiencies are most frequent, and how a simple model flags high-severity risk.

View Full Notebook CMS Data Source

Questions of Interest

  1. Which states see the highest volumes of fire safety citations?
  2. What are the most common safety issues flagged during inspections?
  3. Can we predict whether a new violation may escalate into high severity?

Question 1: Which states see the highest volumes of fire-safety citations?

Answer: The notebook's top-15 chart shows California and Ohio at the top, followed by large-state clusters such as Texas, New York, and Pennsylvania.

Question 2: What are the most common safety issues flagged during inspections?

Answer: Smoke deficiencies are the largest category in the notebook output, with gas, vacuum, electrical, and sprinkler-related deficiencies appearing frequently behind it.

Question 3: Can we predict whether a citation will become high severity?

Answer: The severity distribution is highly imbalanced, and the model captures some high-risk cases while missing others, so recall matters more than accuracy alone.

Takeaway

Data-informed oversight can shift safety management from reactive enforcement to proactive prevention. Combining frequent deficiency patterns with predictive risk scoring supports earlier intervention where residents may face elevated harm.

CRISP-DM Summary

  1. Business/Data Understanding: Identify major risk drivers in nursing home fire-safety citations.
  2. Exploratory Data Analysis: Compare citation volume by state and deficiency category.
  3. Modeling: Train a class-balanced Random Forest pipeline.
  4. Evaluation: Review accuracy with special focus on recall for rare high-severity outcomes.
